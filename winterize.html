Why winterizing matters

Any water left in an RV’s plumbing when the temperature drops below freezing can do real damage.

Water expands when it freezes

Water that freezes inside a pipe, fitting, pump or water heater pushes outward hard enough to crack it.

The damage hides until spring

Cracked lines and fittings often don’t show until you fill up for the first trip and find water inside the walls or under the floor.

Utah freezes early, and harder up high

Nights drop below freezing along the Wasatch Front through the fall and winter, and earlier and colder in Park City, Heber and Ogden Valley.

Freeze damage is expensive and avoidable

Replacing a water heater, pump or hidden lines costs far more than winterizing, and many warranties don’t cover freeze damage.

The winterizing checklist

Every step Mike does on every rig. Doing it yourself? Use this as your list, and call Mike if you get stuck.

  1. 1. Drain the fresh water tank

    Open the tank drain and let it empty completely, so there’s no standing water to freeze and split the tank.

  2. 2. Dump and flush the holding tanks

    Empty the black and gray tanks at a dump station or sewer hookup, and rinse the black tank so waste doesn’t freeze into the valves.

  3. 3. Drain the water heater

    With the heater off, cool and depressurized, pull the drain plug or anode rod and let the tank empty. Mike checks the anode rod while it’s out.

  4. 4. Open the low-point drains and every faucet

    Hot and cold, kitchen, bath, shower and outside shower, so water can run out of every line.

  5. 5. Bypass the water heater

    Set the bypass valves so antifreeze goes through the lines instead of filling the water heater tank.

  6. 6. Blow out the lines with compressed air

    Air pushes the leftover water out of the plumbing, so the antifreeze isn’t diluted.

  7. 7. Pump RV antifreeze through every line

    Non-toxic RV antifreeze goes through the water pump into every faucet, the shower, the toilet and the outside shower until it runs pink at each one.

  8. 8. Protect the drains and traps

    Antifreeze goes down every sink and shower drain and into the toilet to protect the P-traps and tank valves.

  9. 9. Take care of the extras

    Ice makers, washing machines, dishwashers and water filters get drained, bypassed or removed so nothing is left holding water.

  10. 10. Close everything up

    Faucets closed, drain plugs back in, and the water pump switched off.

  11. 11. Batteries and propane

    Batteries are charged and disconnected so they don’t drain over the winter, and propane is shut off at the tank.

  12. 12. A quick look over the rig

    Mike walks the roof, seals and tires and lets you know about anything that could let in water or cause trouble before spring.

Winterizing questions

When should I winterize?

Before the first night below freezing, which usually comes in October on the Wasatch Front and earlier in the mountain valleys. If the forecast is close, don’t wait.

Do I need to be there?

Not if Mike can get into the rig and reach it with his truck. Just arrange access when you book.

What kinds of RVs are included?

Travel trailers, fifth wheels, toy haulers and motorhomes with onboard plumbing. Call if you’re not sure about yours.

Yes. Call or text to set up a spring visit to flush the antifreeze and check for leaks before your first trip.
